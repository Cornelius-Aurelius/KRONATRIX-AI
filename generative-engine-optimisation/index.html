What is Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)?

GEO is the work of making online information easier for generative search and answer systems to discover, understand, evaluate and potentially use when building a response.

GEO in plain English

Generative Engine Optimisation focuses on the qualities that make a page useful as a source: technical access, clear entity information, focused answers, real evidence, understandable relationships and enough context for a system to use the information accurately.

GEO does not mean writing for a mysterious AI algorithm. The strongest approach is still to create genuinely useful source material for people and make the same information accessible and easy for retrieval systems to interpret.

The six practical GEO foundations

1. Technical access

Important pages should return the right status code, be crawlable, use a stable canonical URL and avoid accidental noindex directives.

2. Clear entity

Make it obvious which business, person, product or service the page describes and how it connects to official profiles and related pages.

3. Useful answers

Answer the actual question early, then provide the explanation, limitations, alternatives and next action.

4. Original evidence

Research, real project examples, transparent methods, data and first-hand experience can make a page more useful than a generic summary.

5. Source clarity

Use accountable authorship or publishing information, accurate citations and clear dates where freshness matters.

6. Connected topic structure

Use descriptive internal links so definitions, comparisons, research, services and related questions reinforce one coherent topic.

GEO versus SEO

SEO

Search Engine Optimisation covers the broad foundations of crawlability, indexing, relevance, useful content, authority, user experience and search performance.

GEO

Generative Engine Optimisation places extra emphasis on whether information is sufficiently clear, evidence-rich and source-worthy for generative retrieval and synthesis.

The overlap is large. A page cannot rely on GEO language to compensate for weak SEO foundations. Read the detailed GEO vs SEO comparison.

What makes a page more useful as a generative source?

SpecificityConcrete facts, examples and definitions are easier to evaluate than vague claims.
EvidenceImportant statements should be supported by reliable sources, first-party proof or transparent methodology.
Distinct valueA page should add something useful instead of repackaging the same commodity summary found everywhere else.
ContextGood source material explains when an answer applies, what can change it and where its limitations are.
AttributionThe publisher, business or expert behind the information should be clear enough to identify correctly.
Freshness where relevantChanging platform guidance, pricing, laws and product features should be reviewed and dated rather than treated as permanent facts.

Does Google require GEO?

Google does not require a separate GEO system or special GEO markup for AI Overviews and AI Mode. Google's current guidance says established SEO best practices remain relevant for generative AI features in Search and stresses useful, non-commodity content.

So for Google, GEO is best treated as a strategy layer that reinforces good SEO, evidence and content quality rather than a replacement for Google's documented Search requirements.

GEO mistakes to avoid

Creating pages for every prompt

Scaled near-duplicate pages can create thin content and make the site harder to maintain and understand.

Inventing statistics or authority

Fabricated studies, citations, reviews or credentials undermine the evidence a source is supposed to provide.

Treating schema as a ranking switch

Structured data can support machine understanding when accurate, but it does not guarantee inclusion in a generated answer.

Ignoring the customer

A source that is optimised for machines but unhelpful to people is not a strong long-term content strategy.

Confusing access with selection

Allowing crawlers or submitting a sitemap can support discovery, but it does not mean a page will be indexed, cited or recommended.

Using jargon without definition

The full phrase Generative Engine Optimisation should be clear before relying on the acronym GEO, which has many other meanings.

How to start with GEO

Audit the technical foundationCheck crawling, indexing, canonicals, sitemaps and internal links.
Clarify the entityMake names, services, ownership and official relationships consistent.
Choose real questionsBuild around customer intent rather than arbitrary keyword variants.
Publish a direct answerPut the conclusion first when that helps the reader.
Add evidenceCreate source-worthy material such as original research, real examples, methods, tools or datasets.
Connect related pagesBuild internal links between definitions, comparisons, research, services and next steps.
Measure outcomesTrack indexing, search visibility, AI referrals and qualified business actions where available.
