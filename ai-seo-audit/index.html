What is an AI SEO audit?

It is a structured review of the website factors a business can control across traditional search and AI-powered discovery. That includes crawling and indexing, site architecture, page intent, entity clarity, evidence, source quality, structured information, internal linking, performance and conversion paths.

A useful audit separates technical eligibility from content quality and business outcomes. Being crawlable is not the same as being indexed; being indexed is not the same as ranking; ranking is not the same as being cited by an AI system; and visibility is not the same as generating a qualified enquiry.

What the audit reviews

Technical eligibility

Robots controls, status codes, canonicals, sitemap coverage, crawlable links, indexability, rendering and important page access.

Indexing signals

Whether the URLs intended for search are canonical, internally linked, useful, unique enough to justify indexing and represented correctly in the sitemap.

Architecture

Page purpose, topic overlap, orphan risks, internal linking and whether the site has a clear authority hierarchy.

Intent coverage

Definitions, problem-solving, comparisons, commercial investigation, buying questions and troubleshooting needs relevant to the real customer journey.

Entity clarity

Business identity, services, audiences, ownership, official profiles, locations where applicable and consistency across public signals.

Answer quality

Whether important pages give the direct answer early and then provide enough context, evidence, limitations and next steps.

Evidence and trust

Original research, real examples, authorship, sources, proof, reviews and claims that need stronger support.

Structured information

Whether metadata and structured data accurately reinforce visible content instead of introducing conflicting or unsupported facts.

AI-search access

Relevant crawler controls and source accessibility, including whether search-focused crawlers can reach the pages intended for public discovery.

Conversion

Whether relevant visitors can understand the offer, verify the business and take the next appropriate action with minimal friction.

How an AI SEO audit differs from a traditional SEO audit

Traditional SEO audit

Usually concentrates on technical SEO, indexing, content, keywords, links, on-page optimisation and search performance.

AI SEO & visibility audit

Keeps those foundations, then adds stronger review of entity clarity, direct-answer usefulness, source evidence, AI-search crawler access, attribution and how the website supports modern retrieval-based search journeys.

The two should not compete. AI-search readiness depends heavily on the same strong SEO foundations.

Questions an audit should answer

Can search and AI-search crawlers access the site?Check technical restrictions before diagnosing content.
Are the important pages indexable?Confirm robots directives, canonical signals, status codes and sitemap inclusion.
Does each important page have a distinct purpose?Find thin, overlapping or duplicated pages that may dilute the site.
Is the business easy to identify?Check the entity name, offer, ownership, services, locations and official relationships.
Are real customer questions answered?Compare the site with actual search intent and the buying journey.
Is the evidence strong enough?Identify where original data, project proof, authorship or source citations are missing.
Can visibility be measured?Confirm Search Console, analytics and meaningful conversion tracking where available.
What should be fixed first?Prioritise high-impact, high-confidence issues rather than treating every observation as urgent.

What an audit cannot guarantee

An audit can identify defects, gaps and opportunities in the parts of a website that can be improved. It cannot guarantee indexing, rankings, inclusion in Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT citations, AI recommendations, enquiries, sales or revenue.

The practical value is prioritisation: understanding which problems are technical, which are content or evidence problems, which are conversion problems and which outcomes remain controlled by external platforms.

Useful next steps after an audit

Repair technical blockers

Fix incorrect status codes, crawl restrictions, canonicals, sitemap errors, broken links and accidental index controls.

Consolidate weak overlap

Merge or redirect pages when several URLs compete for the same purpose without enough unique value.

Strengthen priority pages

Add direct answers, unique evidence, better internal links, entity clarity and more complete decision support.

Create missing evidence

Build original research, real case studies, project examples or tools where the business can support them truthfully.

Improve conversion paths

Make the offer, proof, contact route and next action clear once the right visitor arrives.

Measure the change

Annotate important releases and compare indexing, impressions, clicks, referrals and qualified actions over time.
